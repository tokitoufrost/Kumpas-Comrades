A Short History of OPM

Five genres, five different roots: here's where each one on this site came from.

Harana

The Serenade Tradition

Harana dates back to the Spanish colonial era, when a suitor would sing beneath a woman's window accompanied only by a guitar. It stayed alive through the mid-20th century and remains OPM's most intimate, courtship-driven sound.

Pop Rock

The Band Boom

The late 1990s and 2000s saw a wave of Filipino bands (Eraserheads, Rivermaya, and the acts that followed) bring radio-ready guitar hooks and arena-sized choruses into the mainstream.

Bisrock

A Regional Rock Scene

Bisrock grew out of the Visayas' own band circuit, with lyrics sung in Cebuano and other Bisaya languages. It carries the same rock energy as its Manila counterparts, built on regional pride and identity.

Pinoy Hip-Hop

Rhymes in the Vernacular

Pinoy hip-hop took the genre's global template and localized it, mixing Tagalog and regional slang over local beats: a scene that grew from underground battles into a mainstream streaming force.

Indie

The Bedroom Era

Filipino indie grew out of bedrooms and small local scenes rather than major labels, powered by lo-fi production, diary-like lyrics, and streaming platforms that made a DIY release possible nationwide.
